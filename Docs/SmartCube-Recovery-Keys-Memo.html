Keep it locked, without making people start over

We agree on the goal: nobody at SmartCube should ever be able to read a customer's data. This is a way to keep that promise and still let someone who forgets their password carry on.

The two ideas we've had so far

"Lose your password, start over"

Very secure, but everyone forgets a password eventually. Losing years of bills and documents for it is harsh, and it would drive customers away with support calls we couldn't help.

"Send us your database file"

For us to recover anything from the file, SmartCube would need a way in. That way in works for anyone who breaks into us too, and it breaks "we don't hold your data". It also wouldn't work: the password isn't in the file.

The recommendation: one lock, three keys

Each customer's data is locked with a long random key that never leaves their PC. That key is then locked three separate times, and any one of these opens it:

Key 1
Their password
Used every day
Key 2
A recovery key
Shown once, printed or saved, kept by them
Key 3
Their own PC
If they tick "remember this device"
Any one key→data key→bills, profile, documents, bank transactions

SmartCube holds none of the three. This is how BitLocker and password managers such as 1Password and Bitwarden work.

If they forget their password

  1. They reset their account password on the website, as they can today.
  2. On a remembered PC, the PC key opens their data and the app re-locks it with the new password. Nothing is lost.
  3. On a new PC, the app asks for their recovery key. They enter it, choose a new password, and carry on.

Starting over only happens if all three are lost at once: password, recovery key and PC. That is the price of having no back door, and it's the customer's own choice how carefully they keep the recovery key.

Why this is more secure than today

At the moment the files are locked by the customer's Windows login only. This adds their SmartCube password on top, so someone at an unlocked PC, or a virus running as that user, still can't read the data unless the customer chose "remember this device".

What we would build

  • The recovery key at set-up, with a print or save option and a "have you saved it?" check. Existing users see it once on their next sign-in.
  • The three-key lock, with the password turned into a key using a deliberately slow method so guessing is impractical.
  • The bank transaction database locked the same way, which is the one part not yet encrypted.
  • An optional encrypted backup file for OneDrive or a USB stick, opened only by the customer's own keys.
  • The app locking itself after a period of inactivity.

What I need from you

  • Agree the one-lock, three-key design.
  • Agree we never offer to recover a customer's data ourselves, and say so in the terms.
  • Agree the recovery key is shown once and kept by the customer, not by us.

Already in place: account passwords are stored as a one-way scramble, two-factor sign-in is available, all app files and documents are encrypted on the PC, and the server's drive is encrypted.
